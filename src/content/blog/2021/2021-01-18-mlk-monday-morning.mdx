One repeat theme I have for resolutions is being more consistent with writing. The hard part about making this a habit is my perfectionist tendencies flare up around writing, especially if I’m going to share what I write.

So I’ll try to combat this tendency somewhat by trying out a new format, much like Tim Ferriss’s “Five-bullet-Friday” emails. The goal is to write more frequently; if it sparks some discussion - even better.




### New habit I’m trying out

Read daily, finish at least one chapter of a book I’m currently reading. This sounds simple but my issue is that I currently have over 122 books that I’ve started but not finished. This year, I started a shortlist ([Goodreads link](https://www.goodreads.com/review/list/25663968-dong?ref=nav_mybooks&shelf=shortlist)) of 21 books to prioritize finishing. The daily habit also makes sure I continue to make progress on one book at least one chapter a day. With this approach, I already finished 2 books this year - [Siddartha](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/41817978-siddhartha) and [Angel](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/33859095-angel).




### New hobby

Speaking of Angel, I’m re-investing in my hobby of investing by starting with Angel investing this year. I got myself into a couple of Angel syndicates on Angel List and just wrote my first 2 checks this morning to early-stage startups. The approach I’m taking is to use this year as a year of learning and networking as a new Angel investor. I will make ~13 bets into early-stage companies to spread my risk. So far, the best part about this whole process is reading through pitch decks of companies and doing research. If nothing else, it gives my creative juices flowing as I read about new ideas from founders as I evaluate different opportunities. Will definitely write more about this topic as I gain my experience through the year.

### Special guest: A bit of Xu

Speaking of habits, I recommend the book *Atomic Habits*. The key message I got from that book is *getting 1 percent better every day counts for a lot in the long-run*. My belief is that if I find habit-forming is hard, I am not doing it right. Start small, and if it doesn’t stick, make it even smaller. *Consistency over intensity. *Micro-habits (e.g. brushing teeth with one leg standing) can be fun and almost have no cost. If you missed a day or even a week, don’t blame yourself for stopping and simply start again tomorrow :)
