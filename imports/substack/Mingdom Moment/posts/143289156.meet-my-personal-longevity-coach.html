My strategy for delaying mortality is based on two facts:

  1. The vast majority of people (~80%) die from the following 4 causes:

    1. Cardiovascular disease or diabetes. Disease of metabolism

    2. Cancer

    3. Alzheimer's or Dementia

    4. Accidents, like a car crash or falling

  2. Studies in centenarians have shown that the causes of mortality for centenarians are much the same as a normal person. The main difference is that the onset of chronic diseases is delayed by 10-20 years.

So the prominent theory on healthy aging thus far is that we can gain 20 years by delaying the onset of chronic diseases.

Minimizing CVD Risk: A Multi-Pronged Approach

As a physician focused on longevity, I believe minimizing cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk requires a comprehensive strategy that goes beyond simply lowering LDL cholesterol. Here are the key areas to focus on:

1. ApoB and Lp(a) Management:

  • Know your numbers: "When I look at a patient’s blood panel for the first time, my eyes immediately dart to two numbers: apoB and Lp(a). I look at the other numbers, too, but these two tell me the most when it comes to predicting their risk of ASCVD."

  • Lower apoB aggressively: "Simply put, I think you can’t lower apoB and LDL-C too much, provided there are no side effects from treatment. You want it as low as possible."

  • Consider medication: For many, achieving optimal apoB levels requires medication like statins or PCSK9 inhibitors, especially if lifestyle changes aren't sufficient.

  • Don't ignore Lp(a): This lesser-known lipoprotein can be a significant risk factor, especially if there's a family history of premature heart disease. Get tested and manage it through apoB control.

2. Metabolic Health:

  • Address insulin resistance: "Insulin resistance itself is associated with huge increases in one’s risk of cancer (up to twelvefold), Alzheimer’s disease (fivefold), and death from cardiovascular disease (almost sixfold)." Focus on diet and exercise to improve insulin sensitivity.

  • Reduce visceral fat: This type of fat, stored around organs, is particularly harmful and linked to inflammation and chronic disease.

  • Control blood sugar: Aim for stable and low blood glucose levels, ideally around 100 mg/dL, to minimize damage to your vascular system.

3. Lifestyle Factors:

  • Quit smoking: Smoking directly damages the endothelium, the lining of your arteries, and significantly increases CVD risk.

  • Manage blood pressure: High blood pressure also harms the endothelium and should be controlled through lifestyle changes and medication if necessary.

  • Prioritize sleep: "Poor sleep dramatically increases one’s propensity for metabolic dysfunction...and it can wreak havoc with the body’s hormonal balance." Aim for 7-8 hours of quality sleep per night.

  • Manage stress: Chronic stress elevates cortisol, contributing to insulin resistance and increasing cardiovascular risk.

4. Diet:

  • Reduce intake of processed foods and added sugars: These contribute to inflammation, insulin resistance, and weight gain.

  • Focus on whole, unprocessed foods: Prioritize vegetables, fruits, nuts, legumes, and lean protein sources.

  • Consider healthy fats: Include sources of monounsaturated fats like olive oil and avocados, and ensure adequate intake of omega-3 fatty acids.

  • Individualize your approach: Use tools like continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) to understand your body's response to different foods and tailor your diet accordingly.

Remember, it's a marathon, not a sprint. Start by making small, sustainable changes and gradually build upon them. By addressing these key areas, you can significantly reduce your risk of CVD and set yourself on a path towards a longer, healthier life.
