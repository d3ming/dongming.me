I’m sitting here, typing away, stream-of-consciousness.

This is something I struggle with, honestly. On a day to day basis, I struggle with being able to do something with a stream of consciousness that isn’t almost immediately disturbed, by myself.

What is my purpose? I don’t know yet but I suspect I won’t know for a while.

Until I find that purpose, I will resolve to do the following here and now:

  1. Cherish moments of Focus. It’s funny because even as I type this, the act of attempting to cherish a moment makes me lose focus.

  2. Take a deep breath and feel it. No need to fill in all the blanks.

  3. Remember my roots.

The earliest I can remember of my origin, I grew up in Wuhan, China. My earliest memory is that of me walking towards a fire above a gas stove… and I reached and touched it. This seems unlikely to be real since I have no idea how I could have possibly reached the stovetop at age 2. Parents, if you are reading this, please correct this memory for me ;)

Other more likely real early memories include:

  • Hanging on to my mother while she rides the bike to take me to day-care. I didn’t want to go. I never want to go.

  • Standing by a wall to crack open quail eggs, I had at least 2 each morning. This might be the same wall that we used as a ruler to measure my height as I grew. We had many such walls, this might be the first of many.

  • Forcing myself to try to sleep at grandma’s, because I was told as a child that I have to sleep a lot. I could never sleep that much, even back then… but if I appeared to be awake when I should be sleeping, I will surely be punished.

I take these memories for granted, they are a piece of me from a time long gone. There isn’t a single cell in my body today that is from back then. Yet, that was me, and I’m trying to remember my roots…

I grew up, like many of my generation in China, in what would be considered a very humble and modest family. Wuhan is a big city, and we were probably above average in terms of living standards in China. OK, we were probably a couple of standard deviations above average as my grandparents and especially my grandfather was pretty high up in the Communist party.

But this is what I remember from those early days of living at my grandparent’s place which defined my childhood in Wuhan (I stayed there from age 3-6, then from ~9 until I moved to Canada just as I turn 10):

We had a bathtub where we played as kids and washed from. This is the same bathtub that we’d put all sorts of fish and other aquatic wildlife in before they became dinner.

In the same bathroom, there was a really old handle for towels. We didn’t have bath towels, I never even imagined the luxury of bath towels until I came to Canada… what we had in size was what you might call a wash or face towel today. I had 2 towels of the same size which I used for many years, one for my face, one for every other part of the body. That same handle, I later broke on accident on a trip back to China… my dad and I had a huge fight over this because something that was sufficient for my grandma’s house forever is apparently insufficient and easily broken by me. I didn’t get it at the time, because I very much subscribed to the ethos that if it’s so easily broken, it’s clearly not meant to be there. Plus I bet it was cheap to replace.

So yeah, that entire house is actually no longer there, the entire community is no longer there. In its place, my grandmother got a pretty high-end condo unit on the 22nd floor of building A in that community. We lived in a central part of Wuhan, a big city then, a huge city now. Growth is only natural, and we were fortunate to have gotten such an upgrade for free from the government. My grandmother could have chosen a higher floor, she could have chosen a penthouse suite on floor 31 but she chose the 22nd floor. It was good enough. Lagom.

My grandmother is a central figure for my entire family on my dad’s side. She always was for as long as I can remember. If I were to use 3 words to describe my grandmother… I’d say:

  1. Magnanimous

  2. Strict

  3. Smiling. Always smiling. Her face has so many wrinkles from her smiles.

In the years I’ve known her, everything around her changed. She stayed much the same from my perspective. She is perhaps the single most constant figure in my life.

I’m reminded of my grandmother today in my pursuit of minimalism even as I struggle to remember my roots. Today, the 2000 sqft townhouse we live in is larger than my grandma’s home for decades. It’s also a new construction that is filled to the brim with tech that aims to improve quality of life.

If I were to time-travel back to my 6-yo-self and told him that in 30 years I would live where and how I live today, that child would think this is heaven. After all, where he lived, coca-cola was kept bottled up on display, we only got to drink it once a year to celebrate.

My grandmother is the epitome of minimalism. She is adamant about turning off lights and unplugging all electronics. Even today in her new condo. As a kid, I was limited to one TV show a day. Not one show to binge-watch until the next one but one episode of one show, period.

The age of clothes she wears is measured in decades. Do we even make clothes that last this long over here?

I’m reminded of this because the next time I watch a Netflix show or read a book about minimalism or to distract myself as I look for the cure for quiet and focus… I should just think of my grandmother instead.

I just need to think back and remember my roots: I’ve lived with a lot less, I’ve been extremely fortunate to be where I am now, and I can live with a lot less in the future.
